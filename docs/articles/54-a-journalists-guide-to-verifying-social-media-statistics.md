# A Journalist's Guide to Verifying Social-Media Statistics

### This project's own worst citation error is a useful case study in exactly what to check before running with a number

A disputed third-party document claimed 1,706 identical resume-rewrite posts on LinkedIn. This project's independent recomputation against the raw data found 533. That gap is a good template for the specific questions a journalist should ask before citing any social-platform statistic.

## The checklist this project's own errors produced

**Was the number recomputed, or cited?** The 1,706 figure appears to have been repeated rather than independently derived. Any number worth reporting should be traceable to an actual computation against actual data, not a chain of citations back to an unverified original.

**What counts as a "match"?** A duplicate-content count depends heavily on the matching method. Exact string match, normalized-text match, or fuzzy similarity all produce different counts from the same data. A number without its matching method disclosed can't be meaningfully checked or reproduced.

**Does the source show its work?** This project publishes the actual script behind every VERIFIED claim. A statistic with no visible methodology, however credible the source, is a STATED-tier claim at best, reportable as "X says Y," never as an independently established fact.

**Has anyone rechecked their own prior numbers?** This project's own "nine of ten posts show zero views" claim, published earlier in its own commentary, turned out on recheck to be four of ten. A source that has never once corrected its own published work is either unusually careful or hasn't looked hard enough. The second is much more common.

## The general pattern

Every one of these questions reduces to one underlying practice: treat a specific quantitative claim as a hypothesis to verify, not a fact to relay, regardless of how credible the original source seems. The credibility of a source and the correctness of a specific number are two different questions.
