# 2024 vs. 2025-26: How a Year of AI Changed LinkedIn Content

### Career-advice content nearly doubled its share between the older dataset and the newer ones. Technology and AI content grew too, just not as dramatically.

Three datasets, captured at different times, offer a rough before-and-after view of how LinkedIn content mix shifted across roughly a year.

## The datasets, and why this comparison is approximate rather than exact

**podawaa2024** skews earlier. Most of its records decode to dates before October 22, 2024. **HyperClapper** and **LinkBoost-2025** skew later. 45,528 of HyperClapper's 49,369 records (92.2%) and 77,943 of LinkBoost-2025's 77,969 records (99.97%) fall after that same date. These aren't a controlled before-and-after split of the same population. They're three separately sourced datasets from different pod tools, with different user bases and different topic mixes for reasons beyond just time. Read the comparison as directional, not as a clean natural experiment.

## The numbers

| Category | podawaa2024 (earlier) | HyperClapper (later) | LinkBoost-2025 (later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `career_job_search` | 2.23% | **13.92%** | 3.79% |
| `technology_ai` | 16.39% | 27.97% | 25.29% |
| `leadership_coaching_motivation` | 6.15% | 6.16% | **18.91%** |

Technology and AI content share climbed from 16.39% in the earlier dataset to 27.97% and 25.29% in the two later ones, a real and fairly consistent increase across both later sources. Career-job-search content shows a much larger jump in HyperClapper specifically (2.23% to 13.92%) but a far smaller one in LinkBoost-2025 (2.23% to 3.79%), which is itself informative. The "AI content is rising" story is more consistent across datasets than the "career content is rising" story, which looks more like something specific to HyperClapper's particular content mix.

## Why the inconsistency between the two later datasets matters

If both later datasets showed the same jump in career content, that would be reasonably strong evidence of a real platform-wide trend. Because only one of them does, the more honest read is that career-content growth is a HyperClapper-specific pattern, not, yet, a confirmed cross-platform trend. That's exactly the kind of distinction a comparative view surfaces that a single-dataset analysis would miss entirely.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**: all percentages above, independently computed and reproducible from the topic taxonomy.

**INFERENCE**: that the earlier/later split reflects a genuine time trend rather than differences between the three tools' user bases. These are different populations, not a longitudinal sample of the same one.
