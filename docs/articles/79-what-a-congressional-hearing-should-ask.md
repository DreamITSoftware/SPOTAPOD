# What a Congressional Hearing on Platform Manipulation Should Ask

### Not "is engagement manipulation happening." This project already answers that. The harder, more useful questions come after.

If a future congressional hearing addresses social-media engagement manipulation, this project's research suggests the useful questions aren't the ones usually asked in general terms. They're specific enough to actually generate accountable answers.

## Questions this project's data can help ground

**"What share of your platform's reported engagement metrics originates from coordinated, tool-assisted activity, and how is that measured internally?"** This project's own account-concentration findings (a small fraction of accounts producing a large majority of posts, in at least one documented case) demonstrate this kind of concentration is externally detectable from exported data alone, meaning a platform's own internal telemetry should be at least as capable of measuring it.

**"When a third-party vulnerability is discovered in a tool that interacts with your platform, what is your actual notification and remediation timeline?"** This project's LEMPOD case documentation shows at least one instance of acknowledgment and mitigation. A hearing could usefully ask for the general policy and timeline standard behind that instance, not just the instance itself.

**"Does your platform distinguish between different manipulation mechanisms (like-based vs. comment-based, for instance) in your detection systems, or treat all coordinated engagement as one category?"** This project found real, meaningful differences between manipulation models across three separate tools. A single-mechanism detection approach would plausibly miss at least one of them.

## What this project cannot supply for such a hearing

Any testimony, subpoenaed internal data, or sworn statement. This project's contribution is limited to publicly reproducible, aggregate, external-data findings. The questions above are useful because they're answerable by internal data a platform has and outside researchers don't, not because this project could answer them itself.

## What this is, and isn't

**A prepared line of questioning, grounded in this project's VERIFIED findings**, not a claim that any such hearing has occurred or is scheduled.
