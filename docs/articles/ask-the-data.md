# Ask the Data: Series Launch

### A recurring series answering reader-submitted questions against the actual datasets, starting with the two questions this project gets asked most often

Rather than guess what readers want to know next, this series exists to actually ask, and to answer with a real computation rather than a guess, including saying plainly when a question can't be answered from this project's data.

## Question one: "Which topic category has grown the most across your datasets?"

Checked against this project's topic taxonomy comparison, technology and AI content shows the most consistent growth, rising from 16.39% in the earlier-skewing podawaa2024 dataset to 27.97% and 25.29% in the two later-dated datasets, a pattern that replicates across both independent later sources, unlike several other category shifts this project checked that only showed up in one dataset.

## Question two: "Is engagement-pod usage illegal?"

This is the single most common question this project receives, and the honest answer is: it depends on facts this project's data cannot supply. 16 CFR § 465.8 is the most directly applicable rule, and it requires a fake indicator, knowledge it's fake, and use to materially misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose, three elements an aggregate dataset alone can never establish for any specific case. This project can tell you the relevant law and how the data structurally fits its definitions. It cannot and does not tell you whether any specific use of a specific tool was illegal.

## Why this series matters as a format

A research project answering only the questions it chose to ask risks drifting away from what its actual audience wants clarified. Committing to a recurring reader-question format is a structural check against that drift, and it forces this project to be equally honest when the answer is "the data can't tell us that" as when it can.

## How to submit a question for a future installment

Any specific, checkable question about patterns in this project's three datasets, submitted through the repository's usual channels, is a candidate for a future installment, answered with the same computation and evidence-tier discipline as every other article in this project.
