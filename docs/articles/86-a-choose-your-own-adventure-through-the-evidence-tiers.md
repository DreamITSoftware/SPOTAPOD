# A Choose-Your-Own-Adventure Through the Evidence Tiers

### An interactive format for teaching the VERIFIED/CORROBORATED/STATED/UNCORROBORATED/INFERENCE system by making the reader assign the label themselves

Explaining the evidence-tier system in the abstract, as an earlier piece in this series did, is less effective than making a reader actually practice applying it. This format walks through a single claim and asks the reader to choose the tier at each branch point, before revealing this project's own answer.

## The claim to classify

"A LinkedIn post received 88,028 likes while showing zero recorded views."

**Branch one: is this independently reproducible from raw data?** If you said yes, this project directly queried `podawaa2024.json` for this specific record and confirmed both figures, you'd tier this **VERIFIED**. That's correct. This specific claim is VERIFIED.

## The claim to classify, extended

"This specific post's engagement was generated by an engagement pod, not organic activity."

**Branch two: is this independently reproducible from raw data?** This is where the exercise gets more interesting. The honest answer is no. The dataset shows the metric anomaly. It does not show, and cannot show, why the anomaly exists for this specific post. A reader tempted to tier this VERIFIED, because it "obviously follows" from the first claim, has just made the exact category error this whole series exists to prevent. The correct tier is **INFERENCE**, a reasonable interpretation, not a measured fact.

## Why this branch-point structure works better than a definition list

Most people can recite "VERIFIED means independently reproducible" without noticing when they're about to misapply it to an adjacent, less-supported claim. Forcing a reader to actually classify a claim, get it half right, and then see exactly where their reasoning slipped, conflating a verified anomaly with an unverified cause, teaches the discipline in a way a definition never does on its own.

## What this is, and isn't

**A pedagogical format, built entirely from claims already VERIFIED or explicitly INFERENCE-tiered elsewhere in this project.**
