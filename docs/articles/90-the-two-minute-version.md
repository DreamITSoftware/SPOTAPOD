# The Two-Minute Version: This Entire Project, Summarized for Someone With No Time

### Everything this series has covered, in roughly 400 words

Three LinkedIn engagement-pod tools export data describing their own activity. This project analyzes those exports, never LinkedIn's own systems directly, to measure, at an aggregate level, how engagement manipulation actually looks in practice.

## What was found

A meaningful share of posts (35.3% of one 213,491-post dataset) show recorded likes with zero recorded views, a logical impossibility that indicates fabricated metrics. One single post reached 88,028 likes with zero views. A specific resume-rewrite template appears, word for word, across 533 separate posts. In one dataset, the top 100 of 702 accounts produce 83.5% of all posts, a small group doing most of the work. Two of the three tools studied share zero accounts in common, despite serving what should be an overlapping market. A separate documented case shows LinkedIn acknowledging and mitigating a session-credential vulnerability in a third-party pod tool.

## What wasn't found, or isn't claimed

No individual account, post, or person is ever identified. This project's architecture makes that structurally impossible by design, not just a stated policy. No legal violation is asserted against any company or service, even where the data plausibly falls within a specific regulation's definitions. Establishing an actual violation requires facts (intent, a specific misled party, actual commercial harm) that aggregate data can't supply. And this project has corrected its own prior published numbers at least three times, including catching its own overstated "nine of ten posts show zero views" claim and fixing it to the correct figure, four.

## The one habit worth taking away

Every claim above carries a specific evidence tier, VERIFIED, CORROBORATED, STATED, UNCORROBORATED, or INFERENCE, and that tier can only ever be downgraded by later scrutiny, never upgraded. That discipline, more than any individual statistic, is this project's actual contribution. Not "LinkedIn engagement is fake," but "here's exactly how much of a specific claim you're entitled to believe, and why."
