# A Choose-Your-Own-Adventure Through the Evidence Tiers

### An interactive format for teaching the VERIFIED/CORROBORATED/STATED/UNCORROBORATED/INFERENCE system by having the reader assign the label

An earlier piece in this series explained the evidence-tier system in the abstract. Practicing it works better. This format walks through a single claim and asks the reader to choose the tier at each branch point, before revealing this project's answer.

## The claim to classify

"A LinkedIn post received 88,028 likes while showing zero recorded views."

**Branch one: is this independently reproducible from raw data?** If you said yes, because this project directly queried `podawaa2024.json` for this record and confirmed both figures, you'd tier this **VERIFIED**. That is correct.

## The claim to classify, extended

"This specific post's engagement was generated by an engagement pod, not organic activity."

**Branch two: is this independently reproducible from raw data?** The answer is no. The dataset shows the metric anomaly. It does not show why the anomaly exists for this post. A reader who tiers this VERIFIED because it "obviously follows" from the first claim has made the category error this series exists to prevent. The correct tier is **INFERENCE**, a reasonable interpretation rather than a measured fact.

## Why branch points work better than a definition list

Most people can recite "VERIFIED means independently reproducible" without noticing when they misapply it to an adjacent, less-supported claim. Classifying a claim, getting it half right, and seeing where the reasoning slipped (conflating a verified anomaly with an unverified cause) teaches the distinction better than a definition does.

## What this is, and isn't

**A pedagogical format, built entirely from claims already VERIFIED or explicitly INFERENCE-tiered elsewhere in this project.**
