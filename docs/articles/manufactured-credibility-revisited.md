# Manufactured Credibility, Revisited: Series Launch

### The planned rebuttal and companion series correcting the disputed third-party document's remaining unchecked claims, one at a time, the same way the first three were checked

This project's engagement with a specific disputed third-party document began with checking three of its most quotable statistics: a resume-template count, and two account-concentration figures. All three needed correction. This series extends that scrutiny, claim by claim, to the rest of that document.

## Where things stand

**Checked and corrected:** the 1,706 resume-template claim (actual: 533), and the 50.6%/87.3% top-20/top-100 concentration claims (actual: 36.2%/83.5%).

**Not yet checked:** any other specific, falsifiable quantitative claim in the same document that this project has not yet independently recomputed against raw data. This installment does not list them yet, because listing an unchecked claim as a "probably also wrong" candidate would be unearned insinuation.

## Why revisit this document

Three checked claims that all needed correction is a pattern worth taking seriously. It does not establish that every other claim in the document is also wrong, and asserting that without checking would replace one unverified document with an unverified rebuttal. When a source gets three things wrong, the response is to check the rest.

## The standard this series holds itself to

Every future installment in this series checks exactly one additional claim from the disputed document, states the original claim verbatim, performs the independent recomputation, and reports the result, whether that result confirms the original claim, contradicts it, or can't be resolved with available data. A claim that holds up is reported as holding up, with the same visibility as one that doesn't.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED, the three corrections already made.**

**Explicitly not yet claimed**: that any further specific claim in the disputed document is inaccurate. That determination awaits each claim's own individual check.
