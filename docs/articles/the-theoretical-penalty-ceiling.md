# The Theoretical Penalty Ceiling

### $18 billion, one multiplication away, and why that number should never be quoted as real

Take three LinkedIn engagement-pod datasets, 213,491, 49,369, and 77,969 records, and multiply the combined total by the FTC's 2025 inflation-adjusted maximum civil penalty per violation of the FTC Act, $53,088. The result is **$18,093,929,952**.

The arithmetic is correct, but the result is almost certainly meaningless as a legal estimate, for the reason below.

## The assumption behind the number

This calculation treats every single exported record as its own separate, maximum-penalty violation. Nothing supports that assumption. No statute, no published FTC rule, and no enforcement action defines "one violation" as "one row in a spreadsheet." A real penalty calculation would depend on facts no dataset export contains: how the underlying conduct was actually structured, whether it's charged as continuing or discrete, and how enforcement staff or a court chose to count it.

## The more relevant comparison

The useful comparison is what similar conduct has actually settled for, not the ceiling. *LinkedIn Corp. v. TopSocial24 et al.* resulted in a **$43,086** judgment against an engagement-pod-style service in October 2023. That is a citable outcome against a real defendant, using theories in the same family as the ones this project's legal research covers.

Eighteen billion dollars in theoretical maximum exposure, next to a $43,086 judgment for comparable conduct, shows why a back-of-envelope multiplication should never be given to anyone as a damages figure.

## Why this calculation is included

Not addressing "how much would this be worth, from an FTC perspective" would leave a natural question unanswered, and answering with a number and no methodology would be worse. This piece shows the arithmetic, labeled for what it is, so nobody mistakes a multiplication for a legal or financial conclusion.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED, pure arithmetic**: record count × current statutory maximum, reproducible by anyone.

**Explicitly not**: a damages estimate, a penalty prediction, or anything resembling a realistic legal exposure figure.
