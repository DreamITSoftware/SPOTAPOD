# The Veteran-Tribute Template

### One political campaign post inflated a veteran-content count by 25%. Everything underneath it was genuine

Checking LinkedIn for veteran and military-related language turned up 476 raw matches in one dataset. A fifth of them traced back to a single source that had nothing to do with military tribute at all.

## The artifact hiding inside the number

97 of those 476 matches shared the exact same repeated post: a political campaign-endorsement piece describing its candidate as a "decorated 20-year veteran and combat aviator." This is the same template already documented elsewhere in this project's political-content scan. It surfaced here too, simply because the word "veteran" appears inside its text. Corrected for that one repeated post, the real any-mention count is **380**, not 476.

## What's left after the correction is the actual finding

Unlike most duplicate-template corrections in this project, what remained here wasn't noise. It was substantive, distinct tribute content: a fighter pilot's widow continuing his legacy, a soldier's wife honoring her husband, a young army doctor killed saving fellow soldiers, a third-generation officer's family story. These are real people and real tributes, checked individually rather than assumed to be more templated content once the obvious artifact was removed.

## Why this correction matters more than the raw count

A project built on catching inflated numbers has to catch its own inflated numbers too, not just the ones in outside claims. Finding and correcting a 25% inflation in your own veteran-content count, then verifying the remainder is genuine rather than assuming it's all artifact, is the same discipline this project applies everywhere else.
