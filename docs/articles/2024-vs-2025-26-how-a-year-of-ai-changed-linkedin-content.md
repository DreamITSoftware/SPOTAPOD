# 2024 vs. 2025-26: How a Year of AI Changed LinkedIn Content

### Career-advice content nearly doubled its share between the older dataset and the newer ones. Technology and AI content grew too, by a smaller margin.

Three datasets, captured at different times, offer a rough before-and-after view of how LinkedIn content mix shifted across roughly a year.

## The datasets, and why this comparison is approximate

**podawaa2024** skews earlier. Most of its records decode to dates before October 22, 2024. **HyperClapper** and **LinkBoost-2025** skew later. 45,528 of HyperClapper's 49,369 records (92.2%) and 77,943 of LinkBoost-2025's 77,969 records (99.97%) fall after that same date. These are not a controlled before-and-after split of the same population. They are three separately sourced datasets from different pod tools, with different user bases and topic mixes for reasons beyond time. Read the comparison as directional, not as a natural experiment.

## The numbers

| Category | podawaa2024 (earlier) | HyperClapper (later) | LinkBoost-2025 (later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `career_job_search` | 2.23% | **13.92%** | 3.79% |
| `technology_ai` | 16.39% | 27.97% | 25.29% |
| `leadership_coaching_motivation` | 6.15% | 6.16% | **18.91%** |

Technology and AI content share climbed from 16.39% in the earlier dataset to 27.97% and 25.29% in the two later ones, an increase that appears in both later sources. Career-job-search content jumps much more in HyperClapper (2.23% to 13.92%) than in LinkBoost-2025 (2.23% to 3.79%). The rise in AI content is more consistent across datasets than the rise in career content, which looks specific to HyperClapper's content mix.

## Why the difference between the two later datasets matters

If both later datasets showed the same jump in career content, that would be reasonably strong evidence of a platform-wide trend. Only one does, so career-content growth is a HyperClapper-specific pattern and not a confirmed cross-platform trend. A single-dataset analysis would miss this distinction.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**: all percentages above, computed independently and reproducible from the topic taxonomy.

**INFERENCE**: that the earlier/later split reflects a genuine time trend rather than differences between the three tools' user bases. These are different populations, not a longitudinal sample of the same one.
