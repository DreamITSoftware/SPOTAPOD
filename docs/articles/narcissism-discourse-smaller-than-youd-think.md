# LinkedIn's Narcissism Discourse Is Smaller Than You'd Think

### Across 340,829 combined posts, narcissism is mentioned 156 times, and even that number needed a correction

LinkedIn's culture of self-promotion might be expected to generate constant discourse about narcissism, but the data shows little. Combined across three datasets totaling 340,829 records, narcissist, narcissistic, and narcissism mentions total just **156**, before one further correction below even applies.

| Term | podawaa2024 | HyperClapper | LinkBoost-2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narcissistic | 20 | 5 | 116 |
| Narcissist(s) | 9 | 4 | 52 |
| Narcissism | 5 | 2 | - |
| **Any mention (raw)** | 30 | 10 | 116 |

## The correction

LinkBoost-2025's 116 raw matches included 52 sharing one exact string. Checked directly, that repeated post turned out to be a genuine post and not a template artifact. It was a personal recovery and psychedelic-journey narrative describing getting "free from the NARCISSISTS spider web," boosted repeatedly by the platform's own structure and not copy-pasted by many different accounts. Corrected, LinkBoost-2025's real any-mention count is 65, not 116, bringing the combined total across all three datasets down to 105.

## The smallest category found in this entire project

At 30, 10, and 65 mentions respectively, this is smaller than every other content category checked here, including immunotherapy content (16 genuine mentions after its own corrections) and national-security content. For a platform built around individual professional branding, the near-total absence of narcissism as an explicit topic is itself a small finding. It is a useful baseline for how rare a "smallest category" looks in this project's numbers.
