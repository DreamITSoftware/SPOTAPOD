# LinkedIn's Narcissism Discourse Is Smaller Than You'd Think

### Across 340,829 combined posts, narcissism is mentioned 156 times, and even that number needed a correction

If you assumed LinkedIn's culture of self-promotion would generate constant discourse about narcissism, the data says otherwise. Combined across three datasets totaling 340,829 records, narcissist, narcissistic, and narcissism mentions total just **156**, before one further correction below even applies.

| Term | podawaa2024 | HyperClapper | LinkBoost-2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narcissistic | 20 | 5 | 116 |
| Narcissist(s) | 9 | 4 | 52 |
| Narcissism | 5 | 2 | - |
| **Any mention (raw)** | 30 | 10 | 116 |

## The correction that makes it even smaller

LinkBoost-2025's 116 raw matches included 52 sharing one exact string. Checked directly, that repeated post turned out to be genuine, not a template artifact. It was a personal recovery and psychedelic-journey narrative describing getting "free from the NARCISSISTS spider web," boosted repeatedly by the platform's own structure rather than copy-pasted by many different accounts. Corrected, LinkBoost-2025's real any-mention count is 65, not 116, bringing the combined total across all three datasets down to 105.

## The smallest category found in this entire project

At 30, 10, and 65 mentions respectively, this is smaller than every other content category checked here, including immunotherapy content (16 genuine mentions after its own corrections) and national-security content. For a platform built around individual professional branding, the near-total absence of narcissism as an explicit topic of conversation is itself a small, specific finding. Not because it's dramatic, but because it's a useful baseline for how rare a "smallest category" actually looks in this project's own numbers.
