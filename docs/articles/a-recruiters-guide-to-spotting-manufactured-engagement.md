# A Recruiter's Guide to Spotting Manufactured Engagement

### Engagement numbers are one of the weakest signals available for judging a candidate's actual influence or credibility

Recruiters increasingly treat a candidate's LinkedIn presence, post frequency, engagement, follower count, as a soft signal of communication skill or industry standing. This project's data suggests that signal is weaker and more gameable than it looks.

## The patterns worth knowing

Three separate, independently sourced engagement-pod datasets in this project document real, active services built specifically to inflate LinkedIn metrics: coordinated reciprocal liking (HyperClapper, podawaa), and a comment-based manipulation model (LinkBoost) that inflates a different metric than the like-based services do. These aren't fringe tools. The datasets analyzed here span hundreds of thousands of posts and thousands of distinct accounts.

Separately, this project's account-concentration findings show engagement piling up on a small number of accounts. The top 100 authors in one dataset (of 702 total) account for 83.5% of all posts, and posting-velocity analysis found accounts sustaining daily posting streaks over 700 days without a gap, a pattern more consistent with managed or automated content operations than an individual professional's organic activity.

## What this means for evaluating a candidate

A high engagement count on a candidate's posts is not strong evidence of genuine professional influence, and its absence is not strong evidence of the opposite. Content quality, specificity, and consistency of voice across a longer history are more informative than any single engagement number, precisely because engagement numbers are the exact metric these pod services exist to manipulate.

## A caution the other direction, too

It would be an overcorrection to assume every LinkedIn presence with strong engagement is manufactured. Most professional activity on the platform is genuine, and this project's findings describe a real but bounded phenomenon, not a claim that LinkedIn engagement is meaningless across the board. The goal is calibration, not dismissal.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**: the existence, scale, and account-concentration patterns of the engagement-pod services documented in this project's datasets.

**Not established**: whether any specific candidate's engagement is manufactured. This project makes no per-entity claims of any kind.
