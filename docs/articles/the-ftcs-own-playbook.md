# The FTC's Own Playbook: Comparing Your Ceiling Figure to Real Settlements

### $18 billion in theoretical exposure. $43,086 in an actual judgment.

Every enforcement regime has a gap between its statutory ceiling and what gets collected. For engagement pods the gap is large.

## The two numbers, side by side

Multiply three LinkedIn engagement-pod datasets' combined record count by the FTC's current maximum civil penalty per violation and you get **$18,093,929,952**, an extreme arithmetic exercise, built on the unsupported assumption that every single exported record counts as its own maximum-penalty violation.

The real-world comparison: **LinkedIn Corp. v. TopSocial24 et al.** resulted in a **$43,086** judgment against a comparable engagement-pod-style service in 2023.

The theoretical ceiling is roughly 420,000 times the real settlement.

## Why the gap is large

A theoretical ceiling assumes maximum penalty, maximum violation count, and maximum enforcement intent, all at once, conditions that almost never hold in a real case. Real settlements reflect what a court or negotiating parties actually decided a specific set of facts was worth, discounted for litigation risk, proof difficulty, and every element a rule like 16 CFR § 465.8 requires that a dataset alone can't establish (scienter, chief among them).

The ceiling should not be ignored. But a theoretical maximum and a realistic outcome measure different things, and treating one as a stand-in for the other, to make a case look scarier or a risk look smaller, is an error.

## Citing a figure

If you're going to cite a dollar figure about engagement-pod liability, cite the one with a defendant, a docket number, and an actual judgment attached, not the one that comes from multiplying a record count by a statutory maximum. One describes something that happened. The other describes an assumption nobody has charged a case under.

**VERIFIED**: both figures, independently reproducible. One by arithmetic, one by the court record.

**Not equivalent**: a theoretical ceiling and a real settlement are different kinds of number, and neither substitutes for the other.
