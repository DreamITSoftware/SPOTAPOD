# Which Pod Tool Has the Most "Real" Content?

### Comparing distinct-string ratios across three datasets as a rough proxy for how much of each tool's content is templated versus original

None of this project's three datasets are "real" in the sense of being organic, unmanipulated LinkedIn activity. All three exist because someone used a tool designed to inflate engagement. But they differ from each other in how much of their actual text content is unique versus duplicated, which is a separate and informative axis.

## The measure

A distinct-string ratio, the count of unique post text values divided by total post count, gives a rough sense of duplication intensity. A ratio near 1.0 means almost every post has unique text. A ratio well below that means the same or near-identical text is being reused across many records. This project's duplicate-content findings (533 identical resume-rewrite posts, a 400-author single-post cluster, and others) are specific instances of what a low distinct-string ratio looks like up close.

## Why this varies by dataset, and what that suggests

The three datasets' underlying manipulation models differ, like-based reciprocal engagement in two of them, comment-based in the third, and a tool's specific mechanism plausibly shapes how much genuinely original text its user base needs to produce. A like-based pod doesn't require the liking members to write anything at all, which creates room for template-heavy content among the posters themselves. A comment-based model requires participants to produce comment text, which could push toward either more original text (if comments are meant to look natural) or more templated text (if comment generation is itself automated).

## What this comparison is, and isn't, good for

It's a useful way to characterize each tool's dataset at a glance, which one leans more heavily on duplicated templates versus one-off content, without making any claim about which tool is "worse" or more harmful. Duplication rate is a content-pattern signal, not a direct measure of deception or harm on its own.

## What this is, and isn't

**A comparative framework applied to this project's own previously VERIFIED duplicate-content findings**, not a new independent statistic in its own right.
