# The Smallest Category We Found

### Across 340,829 LinkedIn posts, the phrase "rare earth metals" appears exactly 7 times

Rare earth minerals are a major geopolitical and supply-chain topic: export controls, chip manufacturing, EV batteries, national security. A platform built around professional and industry discourse might be expected to reflect some of that conversation. It barely does. Across three LinkedIn datasets totaling 340,829 posts, the specific phrase "rare earth metals," "rare earth elements," or "rare earths" appears a combined **7 times**.

## What did show up instead

Widen the net to adjacent supply-chain minerals (lithium, cobalt, and the general phrase "critical minerals") and the numbers grow, but only slightly: 68 mentions in one dataset, 30 in another, and (after correcting a duplicate-template artifact) 19 in the third. Lithium dominates wherever the topic appears at all, mostly in the context of EV batteries and clean-energy investment, not mining or supply-chain policy.

## The correction that came with this finding

One dataset initially showed 71 matches for this category, driven almost entirely by a single string repeated 53 times. That looked at first like the templated duplicate-content noise documented elsewhere in this project. Checking the actual text showed it was a genuine post about an e-waste and battery-recycling company in India, boosted repeatedly by the platform's own structure. The corrected count for that dataset is 19, not 71. Unlike most corrections in this project, the duplicate here was authentic content, not a template.

## Why the smallest category is worth publishing

Not every finding in this project is about manufactured engagement or inflated numbers. This one is an absence: a topic important in policy and industry circles that has little presence in the LinkedIn content these datasets capture. It is useful context for the other findings, since not every keyword search here turns up a problem.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**, directly reproducible: the exact phrase "rare earth metals/elements/earths" appears 7 times total across all three datasets combined. The corrected any-mention totals (lithium, cobalt, critical minerals, neodymium included) are 68, 30, and 19 respectively per dataset.
