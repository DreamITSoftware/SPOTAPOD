# The Smallest Category We Found

### Across 340,829 LinkedIn posts, the phrase "rare earth metals" appears exactly 7 times

Rare earth minerals are one of the defining geopolitical and supply-chain stories of the decade: export controls, chip manufacturing, EV batteries, national security. You'd expect a platform built around professional and industry discourse to reflect at least some of that conversation.

It barely does. Across three LinkedIn datasets totaling 340,829 posts, the specific phrase "rare earth metals," "rare earth elements," or "rare earths" appears a combined **7 times**.

## What did show up instead

Widen the net to adjacent supply-chain minerals (lithium, cobalt, and the general phrase "critical minerals") and the numbers grow, but not by much: 68 mentions in one dataset, 30 in another, and (after correcting a duplicate-template artifact) 19 in the third. Lithium dominates wherever the topic appears at all, mostly in the context of EV batteries and clean-energy investment, not mining or supply-chain policy.

## The correction that came with this finding

One dataset initially showed 71 matches for this category, driven almost entirely by a single string repeated 53 times. That looked, at first, like the kind of templated duplicate-content noise documented elsewhere in this project. Checking the actual text showed it wasn't noise at all, but a genuine post about an e-waste and battery-recycling company in India, boosted repeatedly by the platform's own structure. The corrected, real count for that dataset is 19, not 71, which is lower than the raw number, but for the opposite reason most corrections in this project go: this time, the duplicate was authentic content, not a template.

## Why the smallest category is worth publishing

Not every finding in a project like this is about manufactured engagement or inflated numbers. Sometimes the finding is an absence: a topic genuinely important in policy and industry circles that simply hasn't reached the volume of LinkedIn content this dataset captures. That's useful context for reading every other, louder finding in this project, a reminder that not every keyword search here is chasing a scandal.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**, directly reproducible: the exact phrase "rare earth metals/elements/earths" appears 7 times total across all three datasets combined. The corrected any-mention totals (lithium, cobalt, critical minerals, neodymium included) are 68, 30, and 19 respectively per dataset.
