# What This Project Predicted Before It Became a Headline [Template]

### A format for tracking whether this project's own inferences turn out right, and when they don't

Research projects often claim they "called it" after the fact. This piece commits to a format that makes such a claim checkable, including the misses.

## The standard this format holds to

Any future entry in this series names the specific prior INFERENCE or hypothesis from this project's published work, quotes it exactly as originally written (not a retroactively sharpened version), and then evaluates whether subsequent events confirmed it, contradicted it, or left it unresolved. A prior inference that turns out wrong gets logged as wrong, in the same format and with the same visibility as one that turns out right.

## Why the wrong ones matter

A list of correct guesses compiled after the fact is a curated sample. Only a list that also includes the misses demonstrates calibration rather than selective memory. This project's "nine of ten" correction, documented elsewhere in this series, is the kind of miss this format would need to include if it had been framed as a prediction rather than a citation error.

## What qualifies as a prediction versus retroactive fit

Only an inference or hypothesis that was published, dated, and stated as INFERENCE before the relevant later event qualifies for this format. A finding restated with hindsight to sound more prescient than it was at the time does not count.

## What this is, and isn't

**A prepared format and standard, with no completed track record.** No specific instance of a confirmed or disconfirmed prediction is claimed here. This piece commits to the format before any specific entry is written.
