# What a "Signal" Is vs. What a "Finding" Is

### One word choice, applied consistently, separates "this pattern is present in the data" from "this pattern proves something happened"

Much bad data journalism comes down to one substitution: treating "we detected a pattern" as the same claim as "we established a fact about the world." This project keeps the two distinguishable, starting with vocabulary.

## The distinction

A signal is a measurable pattern in the data, a keyword rate, a statistical anomaly, a correlation, detected by a specific, disclosed method, with all of that method's limitations attached. A signal does not claim to know why the pattern exists or what it proves. It reports that the pattern is there, reproducibly, at a stated rate.

A finding is a claim about what's actually true, supported by enough independent corroboration that this project is willing to assert it as fact, at whatever evidence tier the support actually earns: VERIFIED, CORROBORATED, or STATED.

Most of what a keyword search, a statistical anomaly detector, or a topic classifier produces is a signal, not a finding. The 1.27% to 3.02% "endorsement disclosure gap" rate discussed elsewhere in this series is a signal: a real, reproducible pattern that does not, on its own, prove any specific undisclosed material relationship exists.

## Why conflating them misleads readers

"We found language patterns consistent with X in 3% of posts" quietly becomes, two sentences later, "3% of posts involve X," and the reader does not notice that the sentence changed from describing a detection method's output to asserting a fact about the world. This happens often in casual data commentary, usually without intent to mislead. The second sentence is more exciting to write, and it is easy to lose track of which claim you are entitled to make.

## How this project tries to prevent it

Every signal-only claim in this project is labeled with a rate, method, and limitation, never as a bare percentage standing in for a conclusion. Every finding-level claim carries an evidence tier that reflects how it was actually established. When a piece is built primarily around a signal (most of the "regulatory signals" content in this project is), its "What this is, and isn't" section says so directly.
