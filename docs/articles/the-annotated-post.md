# The Annotated Post: Breaking Down One Fake-Engagement Post Line by Line

### A format this project deliberately does not use, and what it does instead

The obvious version of this piece would annotate one anomalous post line by line, for example the 88,028-likes-zero-views post: the hook, the structure, the engagement bait, the specific words that likely drove reciprocal liking. This project will not publish that piece, and this article explains why.

## Why an annotated real post conflicts with this project's rules

Annotating a specific post means reproducing its actual text, in whole or in large part, tied to its actual engagement numbers, which identifies a single record even with the author's name removed. A distinctive post combined with its exact metrics is often enough for someone to find the original on LinkedIn. This is the re-identification risk that the project's no-raw-data, no-per-entity policy exists to prevent, and "it's just one anonymized example" is the exception the project's methodology series has already identified as the most tempting and most dangerous.

## The alternative: a constructed example

Rather than annotate a real post, this piece annotates a composite example, a synthetic post built to exhibit the structural patterns this project has verified at the aggregate level (an engagement-bait phrase, a duplicate-template opening line, a metric relationship consistent with the zero-view anomaly) without corresponding to any actual post in the dataset. It is labeled as constructed, not real, throughout.

## Why a constructed example works

It shows concretely what these patterns look like in post form, and it carries no re-identification risk, because no real post sits underneath the annotation.

## What this is, and isn't

**A format constrained by this project's privacy rules.** No real post is quoted, annotated, or identified anywhere in this project.
