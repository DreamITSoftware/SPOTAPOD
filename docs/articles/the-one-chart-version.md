# The One-Chart Version

### The zero-view finding, stripped down to a single visualization anyone can absorb in five seconds

Most of this project's articles carry a paragraph of methodology before the number lands. This is the experiment of doing the opposite: one chart, minimal text, the finding first.

## The chart

A single bar comparison: of 213,491 posts in podawaa2024, **75,442 (35.3%)** show zero recorded views despite carrying recorded likes. Plotted as two bars, "posts with recorded views" and "posts with zero recorded views despite likes," the scale of the anomaly is visible without reading a single sentence of explanation.

## Why this format is worth testing at all

Text-first research writing assumes the reader will follow an argument in order: setup, method, number, implication. A chart-first format bets that showing the number's scale immediately, before any caveats, creates a stronger first impression, and then uses the text underneath to do the caveat work the chart can't.

## The trade-off this format makes

A chart alone can't carry an evidence tier, a methodology note, or a limitation. All of that still has to live somewhere, and stripping the visual down to "one chart" doesn't remove the need for it. It just moves it below the fold. Anyone who shares the chart image alone, without the surrounding text, is sharing a number stripped of the caveats that make it honest. That's a real risk of this format, worth naming rather than ignoring.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**: 75,442 posts (35.3% of 213,491) show zero recorded views despite recorded likes, independently reproducible from `podawaa2024.json`.

**A format experiment**: this piece tests whether a chart-first structure communicates this specific finding more effectively than a text-first one. It doesn't add a new finding beyond what's already published.

Full methodology and the chart's data: **github.com/DreamITSoftware/SPOTAPOD**.
