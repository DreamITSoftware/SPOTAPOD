# 712 Days Without a Day Off

### The top accounts in one LinkedIn dataset sustained multiple posts a day, every day, for up to two years straight

Posting original career content every day for two years without a gap is not realistic for a working professional, and doing it multiple times a day is less so.

Ten accounts in a LinkedIn engagement dataset did it. Across active windows ranging from 174 to 712 consecutive days, these accounts sustained between 1.25 and 3.74 posts per day, every day, for the entire window.

| Account rank | Total posts | Active days | Posts/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,138 | 310.0 | 3.67 |
| 2 | 1,043 | 712.6 | 1.46 |
| 3 | 1,039 | 378.0 | 2.75 |
| 4 | 976 | 310.4 | 3.14 |
| 5 | 801 | 309.2 | 2.59 |

The account at the top of this list posted, on average, once every 6.5 hours, for over ten months, without interruption. The account with the longest active window, 712.6 days, nearly two full years, never dropped below its sustained rate for the entire stretch.

## Irregular timing, sustained rate

One might assume this is a bot: too regular, too mechanical. The data shows the opposite. Every one of these accounts has irregular spacing between individual posts: some intervals close together, some far apart, no fixed rhythm. Whatever produces this volume is not posting on a rigid timer. It sustains an average rate that few individual professionals could, while individual posts still look like a human deciding when to publish.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**, directly reproducible: computing each account's full active window (first post to last) and dividing total posts by that window produces exactly the rates above, for the ten highest-volume accounts in the dataset.

**Not established here**: whether any specific account is run by a single person, a team, or automated tooling. This project reports the behavioral pattern (sustained rate far outside plausible individual authorship) without identifying or making claims about any specific account.
