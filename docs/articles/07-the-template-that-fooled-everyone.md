# The Template That Fooled Everyone

### One exact sentence appears 533 times across LinkedIn's career-advice content

"No replies. No interviews. Just silence for 90 days. Then, I ran my resume through ChatGPT. Got 5 callbacks in less than a week, using just 8 prompts."

That exact sentence (word for word, punctuation and all) appears **533 times** across a combined 340,829 LinkedIn posts pulled from three different sources.

## Why one template matters more than a thousand vague mentions

It would be easy to find thousands of posts that mention resumes, ChatGPT, or job rejection separately, but those are common words, and counting them tells you little. What's different here is that this isn't a topic being discussed independently by hundreds of people. It's the identical narrative arc, the identical numbers, the identical structure, repeated 533 times by what are presented as 533 different individual professionals sharing their personal story.

A rejection-to-success narrative like this one is exactly the kind of post a job seeker is supposed to find credible precisely because it reads as personal testimony. Five hundred and thirty-three "personal" testimonies sharing one script undercuts that credibility at the source.

## What this looked like before it was checked

A third-party document claimed this specific template appeared 1,706 times. That number does not reproduce against the raw source files. The actual, exact-match count is 533, a real and still-striking number, just not the one originally claimed. The 1,706 figure appears to have come from a looser matching method that also picked up related-but-different variants of the story, rather than exact repeats of the same template.
