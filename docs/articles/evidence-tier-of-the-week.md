# Evidence Tier of the Week: Series Launch

### A recurring bit applying this project's five-tier system to real headlines from outside this project entirely, starting with two examples from general tech coverage

This project's evidence-tier system was built for its own data, but the underlying discipline, checking what a headline actually earns versus what it implies, applies just as well to claims this project had no hand in producing. This series exists to practice that in public, on real examples.

## Headline one: "Study finds X% of social media accounts are bots"

A claim like this, without a linked, reproducible methodology showing how "bot" was operationally defined and detected, sits at **STATED** at best, reported as fact by a specific source, not independently verified by the reader or, often, by anyone outside the study's own team. It's not automatically wrong. It's just not automatically VERIFIED-grade just because it's stated with a precise-sounding percentage.

## Headline two: "Company X's engagement metrics quietly declined after policy change Y"

Absent a disclosed before/after methodology and a ruled-out alternative explanation (seasonality, a broader industry trend, a measurement change), this is closer to **UNCORROBORATED** or even **INFERENCE** dressed up as observed fact, a correlation asserted as if it were a demonstrated causal link.

## The actual point of this exercise

The goal isn't to accuse any specific outlet of dishonesty. Most headlines like these are written in good faith, under real space constraints, by people who genuinely believe their framing. The goal is to practice, on material with no stake for this project either way, the exact skill this series' own methodology piece describes: treating a specific claim's evidence tier as a question to answer, not an assumption to inherit from the source's confidence.

## What future installments will and won't do

Each installment picks headlines from public reporting with a testable claim, assigns a tier based on publicly available methodology (never based on assuming bad faith), and explains the reasoning, never naming a source as a means of criticism, only as an example for practicing the skill.

## What this is, and isn't

**A methodology-practice series**, not a fact-check of any specific outlet's overall credibility.
