# The 41,000-to-1 Ratio

### A single LinkedIn post logged 41,315 likes against exactly one recorded view

At a typical engagement rate for a popular post (maybe one in twenty viewers likes it), this post would have needed to be seen more than 800,000 times to generate that many likes honestly. LinkedIn's own view counter says it was seen once.

## Why this ratio is harder to explain

The zero-view posts covered elsewhere in this dataset can be attributed to a view counter that glitched to zero. A single recorded view is harder to explain away, because it means the counting mechanism was working. It recorded one view, nowhere near enough to explain 41,315 likes.

This is the highest likes-to-views ratio found in a 213,491-post dataset of 2024 LinkedIn activity, past 41,000 to 1. The vast majority of posts in the dataset show like-to-view ratios most people would recognize as normal.

## What normal looks like

A typical engaged LinkedIn post might see somewhere between 2% and 8% of its viewers leave a like. A post with exceptional, algorithm-favored reach might climb higher. Ordinary human engagement behavior does not produce a ratio measured in the tens of thousands.

## What this is, and isn't

**VERIFIED**, directly reproducible: this record (41,315 likes against 1 recorded view) exists in the source dataset and can be found by anyone who reruns the published scan.

**Not established here**: the cause. This project does not assert that this specific post was manipulated by any specific tool or actor, only that the numbers, as recorded, describe an engagement pattern with no plausible organic explanation.
