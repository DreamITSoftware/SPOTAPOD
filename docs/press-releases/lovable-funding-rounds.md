# SPOTAPOD Research Finds 118 Pod-Boosted LinkedIn Posts Naming Lovable Across Its Three Funding Rounds

Press release by Dream IT Software. Published on Cyber Defense Wire on 5 November 2026:
<https://cyberdefensewire.com/spotapod-research-finds-118-pod-boosted-linkedin-posts-naming-lovable-across-its-three-funding-rounds/>

The text below is the release as published. The Cyber Defense Wire page is the published version.

---

*Two posts tagged #LovablePartner carried the tag at the end of the post, and a pod account engaged with each within 28 minutes. The data does not show who ordered the pod engagement.*

Daniel Hall, creator of the open-source SPOTAPOD research project, has released an analysis of engagement-pod logs around Lovable, the Swedish AI company whose valuation rose from $1.8 billion in July 2025 to $13.3 billion in August 2026. From August 2025 to October 2026, 118 LinkedIn posts promoting or discussing Lovable went through engagement pods, which logged 1,343 likes and 570 comments on them.

The analysis uses pod logs from three tools (HyperClapper, LinkBoost-2025 and a 2026 file) that are part of the SPOTAPOD research.

Boosted posts naming Lovable appear in 13 of the 16 months from July 2025 through October 2026, around the Series A (July 17, 2025, $200 million at $1.8 billion), the Series B (December 18, 2025, $330 million at $6.6 billion) and the Series C (August 12, 2026, $400 million at $13.3 billion). March 2026 was the peak month, with 17 posts. No Lovable posts were logged in July or August 2026.

"Through the Series A, B and C, some of the LinkedIn enthusiasm for Lovable was manufactured, including on two posts labeled as Lovable partner content," said Hall. "Anyone who reads LinkedIn engagement as a sign of demand for an AI company is reading partly bought numbers."

## Key findings

- **One account's Sunday list, before the Series B.** The LinkBoost pod logged 1,263 likes and 499 comments on eight posts that name Lovable in August and September 2025. One account submitted seven of the eight. Five of the seven are the same AI-tools list, with Lovable at number 16, posted five Sundays in a row from August 10 to September 7, 2025, each time within 11 seconds of 12:00 UTC. The pod gave those five copies 871 likes and 386 comments.
- **No template to catch.** All 499 pod comments on the eight posts differ from each other. Six of them name Lovable, and one of the six rates it "okay."
- **Two partner-tagged posts.** A post tagged #LovablePartner ("So. You've had an app idea.") went up on December 4, 2025, 14 days before the Series B. A SheBuilds build-day post tagged #LovablePartner and #ad went up on March 7, 2026. A pod account liked and commented on them 28 and 5 minutes after posting.
- **A Series B report with its prompt attached.** A post written by software reported the Series B with its AI prompt still on top. It went up on March 2, 2026, 74 days after the December 18 announcement, and a pod account liked and commented on it.
- **Roundups between the rounds.** The 83 boosted posts between the Series B and the Series C are mostly AI-tool roundups. Twenty-two posts outside LinkBoost quote funding or revenue figures that drift from post to post, such as "$100M ARR in 8 months" and "$200M+ ARR."

## Disclosure and legal exposure

Neither partner-tagged post says what the partnership involved. Lovable's formal Partner Program, which pays agencies and independent builders 10% to 20% commission on customers they refer, was announced on September 9, 2026, after both posts. The tags show a claimed connection to Lovable, and the March post's #ad marks it as advertising. Neither tag shows that money changed hands. Both tags sit at the end of the post, and the March post lists it sixth of seven hashtags. The FTC's influencer guidance says a disclosure at the end of a post, or mixed into a group of hashtags, is likely to be missed.

The analysis compares the data with US and EU consumer protection rules. It finds the strongest exposure with the pod operators (16 CFR 465.8(a), the FTC rule on fake indicators of social media influence) and with whoever submitted the two partner posts to a pod (16 CFR 465.8(b) and, in the EU, Annex I point 23c of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive), if the pod order records can be traced. Against Lovable itself, the record supports one theory: that it failed to police its partners' disclosures, and only if it gave these creators something of value. The analysis compares rules with data and is not a legal finding.

## What the data does not show

- **Who paid for the pods.** Nothing in the data links a pod order to Lovable, and most of these posts come from people writing about the company.
- **Payment.** Nothing shows what, if anything, the authors of the two partner-tagged posts received.
- **Employees.** No account that submitted a boosted post lists Lovable as its employer.
- **Coverage.** The logs come from three pod tools. Other tools, and posts nobody logged, are outside the count, so 118 posts is a floor.
- **Effect.** The data cannot show that any investor, reporter or buyer acted on the engagement.

## About the data

The analysis uses pod logs from HyperClapper (49,369 records), LinkBoost-2025 (77,969 records) and a 2026 file (30,504 records). Every record whose post text contains "lovable" was pulled, and posts that use the word in its ordinary sense were read and removed. Each post's date is decoded from its LinkedIn ID, and spot checks matched the tools' own timestamps to the second. Counts and dates can be rerun from the files.
